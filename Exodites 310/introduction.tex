\section{History of the Exodites}

When Eldar civilisation neared its end, a number of Eldar groups denounced the decadence born of Chaos and left their home worlds in a series of emigrations known as the Exodus, calling themselves the Exodites.

Those who joined the Exodus came from every layer of Eldar society, but they were united in their determination and in a powerful will to survive. Most travelled east, crossing the galaxy as far from the main concentrations of Eldar population as they could. The Exodites understood the dangers of hedonism, and so they chose to settle worlds where life would be hard, in order to avoid the trap of idleness. Among the Exodites, the Eldar nature is expressed as fierce loyalty to one's clan and a strong determination to achieve one's goals.

The struggle to survive on these worlds is difficult. To face those conditions, some Exodites converted their graceful war machines into working tools so that they could keep herds of gigantic animals called Megadons. A warrior elite gradually developed a system of rank and honour. Exodite society returned to a near-barbaric state, following the herds across the plains in an endless hunter-gatherer existence, hiding its advanced technology behind a primitive way of life.

In time the craftworlds sought out the Exodites and offered them a place aboard. The Exodites answered their brothers coldly and preferred to remain where life is simple, harsh, and full of danger. Although the craftworlds and the Exodite colonies trade with one another, the Exodites maintain that the easy life of the craftworlds is dangerously close to that which led their race to the Fall.

The Eldar of the craftworlds regard the Exodites as rustic and simple, but also vigorous and savage, very different from their own introverted society.

The Eldar Path determines the way of life of every craftworld, but not that of the Exodites. For this reason they seem wild and individualistic, far more independent of mind and adventurous than their cousins. More importantly, Exodite societies are more rigorous and physical than those of the craftworlds. Where the latter cling to the past and preserve all they can of their fallen civilisation, the Exodites have turned their backs on ancient tradition for a simpler but harder life. Their minds are stricter and more rigid, but also less subtle and perhaps less powerful than those of the craftworlds. Even so they have survived, and of all the Eldar they have the best chance of continuing as they are.
