\section{History of the Necrons}

A terrifying race is slowly awakening across the galaxy. Its members have lain in hibernation in stasis crypts for entire ages, waiting for the galaxy to recover from its ills. Now, after more than sixty million years of absence, their great work may begin. On desolate worlds thought devoid of life, ancient machines stir again and activate reanimation protocols so that the beings in their charge may once more walk among the stars. The dread Necrons have returned, to the galaxy's great misfortune.

Every Necron, from the humblest warrior to the greatest lord, is driven by a single purpose: to restore the glory of their ancient dynasties by re-establishing their hegemony over the galaxy. Such was the edict encoded into the mind of every Necron millions upon millions of years ago, and it is now so deeply implanted that they simply cannot ignore it. It is, however, a task almost impossible to complete, for the galaxy has changed greatly since the Necrons left it to plunge into a long sleep in the depths of their Tomb Worlds. Many of their refuge planets have been destroyed by cosmic disasters or xenos invasions. Others have suffered grave failures, and their buried legions are afflicted with madness or have been irreparably damaged by the irresistible touch of entropy. Degenerate races now inhabit the ruins of some Tomb Worlds, unaware of the remnants of the grandiose civilisation they profane by their mere presence. Unfortunately for them, their ignorance will not protect them when the Necrons come to reclaim what they consider theirs\ldots

If billions of Necrons have been defeated by the passage of time, still more have survived in order to restore their ancient domains. They are no longer creatures of flesh and blood, but android warriors whose immortal form is forged from living metal. Thanks to it they have become almost indestructible, for their bodies can repair and regenerate all damage. Severed limbs can be reattached, armour plates rewelded, and dislocated mechanical organs reconstituted. The only way to finish a Necron is to inflict such damage that its self-repair systems cannot undo it. In the event of irreparable damage, the Necron dematerialises: teleport beams return it to its stasis crypt, where it remains until the damage can be treated by systems still more sophisticated than those of its body.

The technology that allows such wonders remains a mystery to the other races, for the Necrons do not share their secrets and have put protocols in place to prevent them from falling into foreign hands. Indeed, if a Necron fails to dematerialise, it self-destructs in an emerald fireball. Victory against the Necrons is a concept that suddenly seems quite abstract, for a battle turning in the enemy's favour is in no way a firm and final triumph. For the Necrons, defeat is only a minor inconvenience, the prelude to future successes. Immortality has made them patient, and having survived the perils of ancient times has proved to them that with will, their race can overcome every trial. And if there is one thing the Necrons do not lack, it is a will as solid as adamantium. The other races have only a faint hope of resisting the silent legions that rise from their tombs, by preventing their awakening and stopping the Tomb Worlds from uniting, lest the great powers of the galaxy sink to the profit of the Necron dynasties.

Since the first signs of Necron activity, it has never been possible to capture even a single specimen for examination in the laboratories of the Adeptus Mechanicus. Nevertheless, thorough investigations by the Ordo Xenos with the support of specialised Deathwatch teams have established the following facts. It is impossible to recover even the most damaged Necrons because of a powerful internal \textbf{Deep Strike (1)} system that invariably returns them to their catacombs. That is why the only concrete information we possess to date on these beings resides in video recordings.

In general, Necrons of a particular type are all identical, apart from minor details such as their insignia or their degree of oxidation, which may vary according to their world of origin. Necron Warriors, by far the most common, are humanoid in form: bipeds with arms ending in articulated fingers that allow them to handle strange weapons. They share many similarities with the human body, such as joints at the shoulders and hips, thumbs, and a flexible spine. We know that their resemblance to the human skeleton is not accidental: they convey the image of death, probably in order to instil fear and panic in enemy ranks. Their appearance therefore leads one to think that the Necrons exist only to spread war and terror through the galaxy. Necrons are hunched, so that their carapace-shaped shoulder blades reach the level of their heads. They walk slightly crouched, which gives them a morbid gait accentuated by their deathly silence. Some Necrons have been observed moving with ease, while others advance in a jerky manner. It has been suggested that this difference is directly linked to how long these beings have been active.

Necrons are invariably metallic. For the moment we can only speculate as to the nature of the metal of which they are made, but it seems that even if it does not have the resistance of the best Imperial alloys, it is quite capable of absorbing numerous impacts, including Heavy Bolter bursts. The protection it confers also extends to other domains: Necrons are insensitive to conventional toxins and to extreme temperatures. They can moreover operate in the vacuum of space and appear to possess no nervous system. By their nature, it seems likely that they would prove vulnerable to attacks based on concentrated acid and to intense electromagnetic discharges. This last point remains a hypothesis, and members of the Ordo Xenos conduct experiments on the subject at every opportunity.
