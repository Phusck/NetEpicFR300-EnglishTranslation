\section{History of the Exodites}

When Eldar civilisation neared its end, a number of Eldar groups denounced the decadence born of Chaos and abandoned their homeworlds in a series of migrations known as the Exodus, calling themselves the Exodites.

Those who joined the Exodus came from every layer of Eldar society but were united in their determination and their powerful will to survive. Most travelled east, crossing the galaxy as far from the main Eldar population centres as they could. The Exodites were aware of the dangers of hedonism, and so they chose to settle worlds where life would be hard, so as to avoid the trap of indolence. Among the Exodites, the Eldar nature is expressed as a powerful loyalty to one's clan and a strong determination to achieve one's goals.

The struggle to survive on these worlds is difficult. To cope with these conditions, some Exodites converted their graceful war machines into working tools in order to tend herds of gigantic animals called Megadons. A warrior elite gradually developed a system of rank and honour. Exodite society has returned to a near-barbaric state, following the herds across the plains in an endless hunter-gatherer existence, concealing its advanced technology behind a primitive way of life.

In time, the craftworlds sought out the Exodites and offered them a place aboard. The Exodites answered their brothers coldly and preferred to remain where life is simple, harsh, and danger is ever-present. Although the craftworlds and Exodite colonies trade with one another, the Exodites maintain that the easy life of the craftworlds is dangerously close to that which led their race to the Fall.

Craftworld Eldar regard the Exodites as rustic and simple, but also vigorous and savage, quite unlike their own introverted society.

The Eldar Path determines the way of life for every craftworld, but not for the Exodites. For this reason they seem savage and individualistic, far more independent of mind and adventurous than their cousins. More importantly, Exodite societies are more rigorous and physical than those of the craftworlds. Where the latter cling to the past and preserve all they can of their fallen civilisation, the Exodites have turned their backs on ancient tradition for a simpler but more difficult way of life. Their minds are stricter, more rigid, but also less subtle and perhaps less powerful than those of the craftworlds. Yet they have survived, and of all Eldar they have the greatest chance of continuing to do so.
