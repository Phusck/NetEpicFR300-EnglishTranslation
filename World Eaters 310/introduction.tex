\section{The Red Butchers}

Angron's history is incomplete: it contains many gaps, and so many of the events that concern him have become legends that it is impossible to separate truth from falsehood. The world on which Angron grew up is ruled by a caste of elites living in opulence and decadence, while the rest of the population lives in poverty. To distract the poor and satisfy their thirst for blood, the ruling caste organises death-combats in colossal arenas where cybernetically modified gladiators regularly fight.

Angron was discovered by chance by a slave trader ranging through the northern mountains. The Primarch was at the point of death, surrounded by a great number of Aeldari corpses --- they had attacked him in an attempt to prevent what the future had revealed to them concerning him. Though grievously wounded, Angron was still alive. The trader realised what a formidable warrior he was and took him home among his slaves. He was treated, and neuro-biological implants were fitted into his cerebral cortex. These relics of the Dark Age of Technology can increase a warrior's aggressiveness and strength. Angron's potential was quickly recognised, and he was made to fight in the city's most renowned arenas.

After only a few months, Angron had carved out a solid reputation as a warrior. He killed countless gladiators, though he always spared those who had fought well, which earned him the favour of the crowds. Angron constantly sought to escape his condition as a slave and caused his gaolers a great deal of trouble. He tried several times to escape the arena prisons. But the gladiators' surveillance systems were numerous and reinforced by hundreds of heavily armed guards, so that every attempt at evasion was doomed to fail.

After a few years, his reputation as a gladiator had become immense, and crowds rushed to watch his combats. Under his tutelage, his gladiator brothers grew ever stronger, so that the warriors of no other arena could rival them. His inflexible code of honour and his training methods earned him the respect and trust of the other gladiators. As the great games at the end of the year approached, he prepared his most daring escape. Those games were to include a general melee involving every gladiator in the arena. At the height of the fighting, while the crowd roared with joy, Angron and his companions turned on the guards and massacred them in order to cut a path to the exit.

Two thousand of them managed to escape the city, stealing every weapon and every store of food they could lay their hands on before fleeing into the mountains. Angron's new army took refuge in the northern peaks, very near the place where he had been found a few years earlier. The planet's rulers sent army after army to dislodge them, but Angron's command was such that all failed. The slaves nevertheless suffered from hunger and privation and were finally surrounded by five armies far superior in number. Even the Primarch could not hold against so many opponents. It was then that the Emperor came to him, guided by his psychic aura. He had secretly observed his son from his ship and was very proud of him as he watched him lead his men in battle. He then descended to the planet to offer Angron command of the War Hounds Space Marine Legion.

To the Emperor's great surprise, Angron refused. His place was at his companions' side, and he preferred to die rather than abandon them. The Emperor knew that, Primarch though he was, Angron would lose his life in the fighting to come, and so He teleported His son onto His ship before the worst could happen. Deprived of their leader, the slaves were massacred. Angron took part in the Great Crusade at the head of the newly renamed World Eaters, but he would never forgive his father for what he considered a betrayal of his honour.

Aware of the effectiveness of cerebral surgery on warriors' combat prowess, Angron ordered his Legion's Techmarines to use his own implant as a model so that his men might benefit from it. Little by little, the Legion's use of psychosurgery became systematic. Under its influence they perpetrated a massacre on Ghenna in which the entire population of the planet was slaughtered in a single night. The World Eaters were recalled to order by the Emperor, who commanded that the implants cease to be used, but Angron paid no attention.

It became common among the World Eaters to count the number of skulls taken during battles and to make a true competition of it. Many other Primarchs expressed their consternation to the Emperor, pushing the new Father of Mankind to make a fatal error by sending Horus to meet Angron and bring him back to the right path. Horus was a fine psychologist and, above all, he had already secretly turned to Chaos. Horus found in Angron a bitter warrior, consumed by resentment, and it was therefore easy to use his weaknesses to paint him a picture of a weak Emperor, quick to betray and needing to be replaced as soon as possible. He told Angron everything the latter wanted to hear, so that he turned against the Emperor.

The World Eaters then abandoned the Emperor's enemies and turned their ferocity and hatred against the Imperium. As was their habit, they were found in the front line of every important battle, preferring to butcher their opponents in close combat rather than employ long-range weapons. They cut a bloody path across the galaxy as far as Terra, drinking the blood of their victims and taking their skulls in honour of their new master, Khorne, the Blood God. On Terra, the World Eaters took possession of the ground, led by the corrupted form of Angron brandishing his incandescent rune-sword. Despite their devastating assault, final victory was snatched from them. At the approach of the Dark Angels and Space Wolves Legions, Horus precipitated events. He lowered the defences of his barge in order to provoke the Emperor into single combat. The latter took up the challenge and engaged the traitor in a confrontation that would decide the fate of the galaxy. The two opponents fought in every dimension --- physical, spiritual, and psychic --- until the Emperor finally killed Horus. Deprived of its commander, the Chaos host disintegrated and fled Terra. Angron was the last to leave, and he led his Legion into the heart of the Eye of Terror.
