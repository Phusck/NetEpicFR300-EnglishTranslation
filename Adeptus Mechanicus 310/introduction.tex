\section{The Cult of the Machine}

In the 41st Millennium, it is said that the mighty Imperium of Mankind spans a million worlds. The Emperor-God's vast fleets and armies, composed of billions of men, wage an unending war against xenos, traitors, and heretics, from the Halo Stars to the galactic core. The Imperium itself is a grim and unyielding authority in which any form of autonomy is a great privilege. Within the Administratum of the Adeptus Terra, departments exist to supervise, watch, and control the countless souls of the Imperial domain. Ten thousand years of civil wars, heresies, pogroms, and coups have created a bureaucracy that is extremely wary of the dangers of granting too much independence to its citizens, whether Planetary Governors or mere serfs.

There is, however, one organisation in the Imperium that stands apart and is practically independent of the Adepts of Terra. The red-robed agents of this organisation appear on every Imperial world and aboard every one of its ships. It possesses entire worlds under its sole authority, controls practically every installation the Imperium needs to wage war, and yet retains unprecedented autonomy and power. That organisation is the Adeptus Mechanicus, and its history is as long and tumultuous as that of the Imperium.

In an age when so much knowledge was lost, it is the Adeptus Mechanicus that provides the Imperium with its technical expertise. The Tech-Priests manufacture and maintain a myriad of essential machines, from the most powerful plasma reactors to the humblest vox, and even the Golden Throne. The Tech-Priests are those who preserve the knowledge of past ages. By Imperial decree, every new discovery belongs to the Tech-Priests, whether a recovered strongbox or a rediscovered starship.

The Adeptus Mechanicus's authority in matters of technology is unquestioned. The ordinary Imperial citizen would consider it sacrilege to misuse a machine deliberately, and believes that departing from the prescribed rites of maintenance leads to discord with a machine's wrathful spirit. The Tech-augurs of the Adeptus Mechanicus are present in every fleet and every expeditionary force, while the Tech-Priests tend the essential machines of the Imperium on every world and every vessel. The Forge Worlds have become essential centres of manufacture and trade for entire sectors of space. The Adeptus Mechanicus's legions of war machines are fully maintained and committed to the punishment of Humanity's enemies. Even the mighty Adeptus Astartes send their own to Mars to learn the secrets of the Machine and train as Techmarines. Without the Adeptus Mechanicus, the Imperium would doubtless collapse.

Perhaps because of that very importance, some of the Adeptus Mechanicus's autonomy eroded gradually over the millennia. The events of the Horus Heresy and the convulsions that followed in the Imperium have subjected the Forge Worlds to inquisitorial scrutiny at a level unimaginable since the earliest days of the Treaty of Olympus. Fear of accusations of heresy has limited the safe exchange of ideas. True knowledge was gradually replaced by rote learning and understanding by dogma.

In hidden places, far from the rigours of the Mechanicum, some hold that the Cult of the Machine's greatest heresy is to consider that only the oldest technology is worthy of study and use. The Tech-Priests continue to analyse, catalogue, and examine every fragment they find, but in the rare cases where they discover an example of innovation, it is often dangerous to its operators. The Cult Mechanicus treats every form of innovation with supreme scepticism, and some claim that knowledge of the machine has been supplanted by mere worship of its function. As a result, with the exception of a few notable individuals, there are few Tech-Priests in the 41st Millennium who can approach the technical genius of their predecessors. With each century, a little more of the technological lore accumulated by Humanity passes beyond understanding and becomes myth and legend.

Thanks to the Cult Mechanicus, the Imperium's war machines are able to fight simultaneously on a million planets. The number of the Omnissiah's faithful is incalculable, and its mastery of machines is felt in every armed force of Humanity, from remote garrisons to the legions of divine machines.

A great part of the weapons used by the Imperium's regular armies originates from an Adeptus Mechanicus Forge World. Only with the Omnissiah's assent do thousands of Astra Militarum tank squadrons tread the battlefields, Imperial Navy armadas carry crusade armies across the stars, and Adeptus Astartes strike forces fall from the heavens sowing death. Even the Emperor owes his survival only to the knowledge of the Adeptus Mechanicus, for the Tech-Priests alone know the secrets of the Golden Throne. They are aware of the vital place they occupy within the Imperium, yet they reserve for themselves the privilege of wielding the most devastating weapons, studying the most unfathomable mysteries, and committing the most atrocious acts.

More than a vital organ of Humanity's galactic empire, the Cult Mechanicus obeys its own creed, older than the Imperium itself. During the ages when Mars was cut off from Terra by supernatural storms, its technocrats came to worship technology. Despite the ravages of ten thousand years of war, their unshakeable faith has allowed them to survive. It is that faith that ensures the power of the Omnissiah's armies, and that instils in them a religious fervour that crackles literally upon the bodies of warriors and machines.
